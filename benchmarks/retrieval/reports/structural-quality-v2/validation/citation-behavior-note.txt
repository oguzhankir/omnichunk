Markdown paragraph isolation deliberately changes packing even when an entire small document fits the maximum. A document with a heading and three plain paragraphs can now produce three chunks at a 256-character capacity, rather than one combined chunk. A heading can accompany its following paragraph when the complete source payload fits. Distinct body paragraphs retain separate retrieval identities, and the soft minimum does not recombine them. Plaintext retains its existing paragraph packing; the clause/bracket changes affect the recursive fallback needed for oversized prose.

This behavior prioritizes narrower retrieval units and stable owning headings. It can increase chunk counts, embedding/index work and stored vectors, and can reduce adjacent context inside each retrieved passage. The implementation adds no compact-profile flag and makes no universal latency, memory or embedding-cost improvement claim. Evaluate these costs alongside fixed-budget evidence gains and losses for the relevant workload.
